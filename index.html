About Him

Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam (1931–2015) was an Indian aerospace scientist and the 11th President of India (2002–2007). He played a major role in the development of India’s space and defense technology and was deeply involved in projects like the Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III) and the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP), which produced missiles including Agni and Prithvi. Because of these contributions, he earned the popular title “Missile Man of India.” Dr. Kalam also played a key role in India’s Pokhran-II nuclear tests in 1998, helping the country become a nuclear power. After his presidency, he continued to inspire young people as a teacher, writer, and speaker, always encouraging students to dream big and work hard.

He was known for his humility, dedication, and love for students. His vision for India inspired millions of young minds.

Major Achievements

  • Developed India's first satellite launch vehicle (SLV-III)
  • Key role in Pokhran-II nuclear tests
  • Received Bharat Ratna in 1997
  • Served as President of India (2002–2007)
